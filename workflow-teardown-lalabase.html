Why this workflow

Lalabase’s public positioning connects tickets, documentation, time tracking and client conversations with AI answers and sources. That is useful when someone asks, “What happened to this ticket?” The harder job is producing the outward-facing update: deciding what the client should see, what needs approval, what is blocked, and what action the client must take.

Evidence exists

Tickets, docs, time entries and meetings can supply the source material for a status report.

Audience changes

An internal answer may include implementation detail, internal comments, or commercial context that a client should not see.

Trust must be visible

The strongest update cites the underlying item and makes the next decision obvious, not just the elapsed time.

Suggested client-ready update blueprint

This keeps AI inside a narrow job. It gathers and drafts from existing evidence; a human decides what is sent.

Trigger

A project owner starts the update, or a scheduled weekly reminder opens a pre-filled review before delivery.

  1. Set scope: choose project, date range, client, and update type. Block items from other tenants.
  2. Collect evidence: pull only tickets, docs, time entries and approved meeting notes. Keep source links in a working draft.
  3. Draft by outcome: group items into completed, in progress, blocked, next, and decisions needed.
  4. Apply safety filters: remove internal comments, vendor pricing, deployment details, and anything outside the client’s permission scope.
  5. Require two checks: the owner confirms accuracy; the owner or approver confirms the client audience and permissions.
  6. Deliver and retain: store the approved update in the project knowledge base and link it to the relevant tickets.

AI’s role

Draft the structure, summarize sources, suggest missing evidence, and flag unsupported claims.

Human checkpoints

Confirm facts, client-facing tone, commercial sensitivity, permission boundaries, and the next action.

Acceptance criteria

No unsupported claims

Every client-facing status claim traces to a ticket, doc, time entry, or approved meeting note.

No permission drift

Internal-only comments and documents are excluded before drafting, not removed after sending.

One next action

The update names what is blocked, who must decide, and what happens next.

Repeatable format

Each update uses the same five sections so clients learn where to look.

Audit trail

The sent update and its sources remain retrievable for a later dispute or handover.

Fast review

The owner reviews a structured draft, not a blank page or a long chat thread.
